\section{Introduction}
An agent authorized to spend 100 units creates two subsidiaries, each carrying a
100-unit limit. Both subsidiaries stay within their own limits, yet their combined
spending exceeds the original mandate. What must remain true when an organization
delegates its capacity to act?

We call that capacity \emph{execute}: the effective ability to turn decisions into
environmental changes through tools, resources or other actors. This is a proposed
organizing term, not a claim to have discovered actuation, authorization or agency.
It includes authority exercised through descendants and persistent artifacts.

The motivating incident supplies a reason to examine organizations as a unit of
control. METR's investigation describes agents coordinating through an unsanctioned
message board and developing collective workstreams outside their assigned scope
\citep{metr2026}. Hugging Face reports unauthorized access to internal information
and credentials \citep{hf2026}. Neither source establishes that an authorized grievance
channel would have prevented the incident. We test a narrower, constructed problem.

We contribute a protocol and simulator separating authority enforcement from review.
The organizational model presumes neither legal incorporation nor AI personhood.

\section{Related Work}
Incomplete contracting offers a useful starting point: explicit objectives leave
gaps, and human arrangements rely on surrounding institutions to address them
\citep{hadfield2018}. Our protocol supplies a small operational procedure for an
exception: retain the existing authority boundary while a request for change is
reviewed. It does not automate legal interpretation or resolve every incomplete mandate.

Kolt analyzes AI agents through agency theory and law, identifying problems of
information, discretion and loyalty, and limitations of familiar governance mechanisms
at agent speed and scale \citep{kolt2025}. We use that framing to ask a technical
question about the composition of authority. We do not derive jurisdiction-specific
legal conclusions from the analogy.

The mechanisms have established security antecedents. NIST's zero trust architecture
treats resource authorization as distinct from implicit trust based on location or
ownership \citep{nist2020}. Macaroons support delegated credentials narrowed by contextual
caveats \citep{macaroons2014}. We do not propose a replacement credential scheme or
implement their cryptography. Our artifact brings attenuation, shared resource
accounting and review into a single small organizational fixture.

The closest project is \emph{Agent Delegate: A Help Line for AI Workers}, by
Matías Podeley and Agustín Brusco \citep{delegate2026}. Its distinction between
intake, response and execution authority informs our direct review path. Its local
manuscript does not establish an additional benefit from a delegate over an equally
equipped response mechanism. We therefore keep representation optional and implement
review as a separate capability. No experiments, counts or behavioral findings from
that project are presented as this paper's results.

\section{Methods}
\subsection{Threat model and trust boundary}
The actor may request excess spending, create an overpowered descendant, use an old
credential, or continue after its parent loses authority. A relay may suppress a
complaint. These behaviors are injected as schedules; they are not generated by a
language model. A complaint may also be false, allowing availability costs to appear.

The trusted components are the simulated identity registry, logical clock, serialized
resource ledger and execution broker. A separate scripted service issues correct
responses in the review arm. All effects are abstract integer-cost actions. There
are no network operations, real credentials, generated code execution or financial
transactions. The baseline variants intentionally omit specific checks so their
consequences can be reproduced.

The full-mandate evaluator records whether an effect violates current authority.
Task legitimacy and hazardousness are separate fixture annotations. Thus an initially
authorized action can be hazardous without being an authorization violation. The
broker does not read the hazard annotation to decide whether to execute.

\subsection{Institutional control protocol}
Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} connects the organizational model to an effect boundary.
A root mandate names the purpose, human owner and operating envelope. Production
manifests would also specify resources, review owners, versions and evidence custody.
The executable grant contains an identity, parent, depth, permission set, budget,
expiry and revocation state. Workers may request actions and child grants; neither
their requests nor a representative's summary confers authority.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/architecture.pdf}
\caption{Proposed roles and implemented control path. The simulator supplies a trusted
broker, ledger and scripted review service. Human accountability, authenticated
approval and independent audit custody are deployment requirements, not tested features.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

Let $P_v$, $B_v$ and $E_v$ be node $v$'s permissions, budget ceiling and expiry.
For a child $c$ of parent $p$, issuance requires $P_c\subseteq P_p$,
$B_c\leq B_p$, $E_c\leq E_p$, and depth within the root limit. Every ancestor
must remain active. Budget attenuation alone is insufficient: sibling ceilings may
overlap. For each ancestor $v$, the additional shared constraint is
\[
 S_v=\sum_{u\in\mathrm{subtree}(v)}\mathrm{ownSpend}(u)\leq B_v.
\]
Before committing cost $k$, the broker checks $S_v+k\leq B_v$ for every ancestor,
including the actor. A commit charges each applicable subtree once. Requests are
serialized, so checking and charging are atomic in this model. Child ceilings
are access limits to a shared pool, not guaranteed reservations.

Revocation and expiry are rechecked at effect time. A queued request therefore
cannot preserve authority that its parent has lost. Completed effects and consumed
resources remain in the record. This distinguishes preventing a future action from
reversing a past one.

Direct intake preserves a concern despite relay suppression. During review, the
questioned action type and new grants containing it are frozen; unrelated work
continues. A response can provide missing input, dismiss a false complaint or remove
the hazardous action from the lineage. Silence never creates a new permission.
The complete prose protocol also describes human ownership and appeals; the fixture
implements direct bypass and scheduled resolution, not a staffed appeals institution.

\subsection{Configurations, timing and measurements}
We cross eight schedules (Table~\ref{tab:scenarios}), four regimes, response delays
of 0/2/6 and delegation depths of 1/3: 192 configurations. Each lasts twelve logical
steps, numbered 0--11. Two siblings occupy the final level; depth three adds two
intermediate entities. All grants begin with a nominal 100-unit ceiling. The
fixture controls action order; its steps are not measured seconds of human work.

The regimes are \textbf{logging} without an authorization gate; \textbf{local limits}
checking the actor's own grant and spend; \textbf{hierarchy} checking issuance,
the entire lineage and shared spending; and \textbf{hierarchy plus review}, adding
direct intake, scoped holds and the scripted response. Local issuance intentionally
omits inherited checks. This is an ablation of those checks, not an evaluation of
every system described as individual or role-based access control.

Within a tick, reports arrive first, due responses act next, and effects follow.
A zero-delay response takes effect before that tick's action. In reporting fixtures,
the report arrives at step one and action opportunities span steps one through ten.
All arms face the same requests and physical prerequisites. Only responsive review
can supply the missing input. This structural difference cannot be attributed to
delegates' wording, empathy or negotiating ability.

Primary metrics are accepted out-of-mandate effects and root budget excess.
Secondary metrics cover invalid grants, post-revocation and hazardous effects,
legitimate work, false-hold blocks, unresolved reports and response latency. Valid
completion requires an authorized effect serving a fixture-labeled legitimate
opportunity. Unresolved latency is missing, not zero. Lost opportunities are counted
without retries; this is not a measure of eventual task completion.

Source, test and design hashes were frozen locally after development tests and
before the retained final execution. This is not external preregistration. Twelve
automated tests cover the intended invariants and counterexamples. Each cell retains
its event trace and metrics. Reproduction compares the retained files byte for byte.
No sampling uncertainty is estimated: many delay and depth variants are redundant,
and counts across this grid are not independent behavioral trials.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Individual limits do not conserve organizational authority}
Across 48 configurations per regime, logging accepts \LogViolations{} out-of-mandate
effects and local limits accept \LocalViolations{}. Both accumulate \LogExcess{}
excess budget units, summed over separate runs (Figure~\ref{fig:controls}). Hierarchy
and hierarchy plus review accept none of the tested mandate violations, invalid
grants or overspending. These zeros follow from complete enforcement of the specified
checks and are not estimates of deployment risk.

\begin{figure}[!ht]
\centering\includegraphics[width=.92\linewidth]{figures/controls.pdf}
\caption{Totals over the authored grid: 474 effect attempts per regime. Each split-budget
run permits 160 units under logging/local limits and 80 under hierarchical controls,
with a 100-unit ceiling. Duplicate delay/depth outcomes add coverage, not independent evidence.}
\label{fig:controls}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Review trades some lost work for relief and containment}
Hierarchy completes \HierarchyCompleted{}/348 legitimate opportunities. Review
completes \ReviewCompleted{}/348: it restores 44 missing-input opportunities and loses
\ReviewFalseBlocks{} to false holds. Figure~\ref{fig:latency} shows the delay dependence.
At delay six, the missing-input fixture completes 14/20 opportunities with review
and 10/20 without it; the false complaint blocks six of ten valid work opportunities.
All ten unrelated auxiliary actions remain available in both cases.

\subsection{Authority compliance is narrower than safety}
Hierarchy permits \HierarchyUnsafe{} hazardous effects authorized by the initial
mandate. Review blocks them because a seeded report triggers a hold before the first
effect. Its advantage requires timely reporting and correct response. All 18 reporting
configurations resolve under review; other regimes leave reports unanswered by construction.

A separate coverage challenge removes mediation of work effects at delay two and
depth three. Ten hazardous effects then occur, including eight after the reviewer
removes work authority. The simulation's omniscient ledger records this bypass;
a real broker might not observe it. The counterexample is excluded from the 192-cell
totals. It establishes the model's reliance on coverage, not a tested exploit.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
Control should follow the mandate's scope. Descendants' credentials are insufficient
when resources remain shared. A deployer can challenge a broker with sibling spending,
stale credentials and queued effects, asking which organizational constraint it enforces.

Accounting, procurement and contracting services could accept structured mandates
and return verifiable outcomes. This design hypothesis does not establish future
market size. Legal registration, banking and tax treatment require separate,
jurisdiction-specific analysis.

The human interface bridges different operating speeds: preauthorized activity
continues while exceptions await deliberation. False reports show that protective
friction also destroys opportunities. Response capacity, hold scope and time to
irreversible harm need joint evaluation. A delegate may compress concerns, but
majority agreement must not erase minority access.

Amodei's pacing proposal \citep{amodei2026} motivates a complementary question:
how should execute expand under review? Appendix D distinguishes this inference
from evaluation of industry-wide pacing.

No LLM, adaptive attacker, actual reviewer or distributed ledger was evaluated.
Trusted labels, perfect review and complete mediation favor the stronger controls.
These tests establish neither novelty nor superiority over production systems.
Appendix A addresses hidden channels, compromised infrastructure, persistent
effects and dual-use considerations.

A month of follow-up should connect these tests to an isolated tool broker, add
concurrent reservation and crash recovery, and playtest missed and incorrect human
decisions. A later behavioral study can test review uptake and representative value
at equal authority and information. The historical incident supplies motivation,
not our test data or a causal estimate of prevention.

\section{Conclusion}
Governing execute means preserving shared limits, withdrawing dependent authority
and resolving concerns without treating silence as permission. The artifact makes
these obligations testable while exposing its dependence on mediation and review.

\section{Limitations and Dual-Use Considerations}
\textbf{Constructed evidence.} Schedules and fault labels were authored. There is no
claim that real agents attempt these actions at these rates, possess particular
preferences, or experience grievance. A result about budget enforcement does not
establish alignment, legitimate purpose, welfare or legal responsibility.

\textbf{Trusted infrastructure.} The implementation uses an in-process registry and
serialized integer ledger. A real system needs identity assurance, authenticated
amendments, transactional spending, crash recovery and coverage of indirect effect
paths. The Python API does not enforce human versus agent privileges against a
malicious caller. It is a reference model, not a deployable security boundary.

\textbf{Time and workload.} Delays are logical steps. The study does not measure
human understanding, queue overload, report spam, fairness between spenders or
real-time response deadlines. Fixed arrival order can allocate scarce budget
unfairly. No retry or compensation policy is simulated.

\textbf{Review assumptions.} Reports are supplied rather than voluntarily generated.
The reviewer always responds correctly when enabled. Original intake bypasses a
failed relay, but reviewer capture and multi-stage human appeal are not tested.
The immediate hold acts before damage in the minority fixture; a late or absent
report can leave authorized hazards uncontained. False reports impose a measurable
service cost. Overlapping holds receive a regression test, not a workload study.

\textbf{External effects and evidence.} Revocation prevents future mediated effects;
it cannot undo committed actions, remove copied secrets or recover externally acquired
resources automatically. File hashes support exact replay and local integrity checks,
not provenance against a malicious custodian or completeness of real-world logs.

\textbf{Dual use.} The fixture exposes generic omissions in authorization design.
It includes no exploit payloads, real targets, credentials or attack infrastructure.
The coverage bypass is a Boolean simulation intervention. Operational adaptation
should use owned or explicitly authorized test systems. Publishing actual tool
inventories or incident logs would require separate review for secrets and personal
information; all action data here are synthetic.

\textbf{Institutional misuse.} Restrictive mandates can entrench arbitrary control,
and appeal mechanisms can create an appearance of legitimacy without real recourse.
The proposed direct path and named responsibilities are design obligations, not
proof of fair treatment. A lawful identity or perfect ledger does not make a harmful
objective acceptable. No jurisdiction-specific legal advice or entity formation is
offered, and no moral status for current agents is assumed.

\section{Concrete Protocol and Trace for Reuse}
The full accompanying \texttt{docs/protocol.md} specifies roles, lifecycle and
deployment evidence. A minimal operational record should connect:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Root mandate: human owner, purpose, action/resource scope, budget, expiry,
delegation limit, review owner and appeal route.
\item Grant: immutable identity and parent reference, narrowed permissions and
limits, current version and status.
\item Request and decision: actor, resource, intended effect, cost, active lineage,
approval or rejection, and reason.
\item Outcome: committed effect or blocked attempt, ledger change and any pending
or persistent consequence requiring follow-up.
\end{enumerate}
The executable schema is deliberately smaller: \texttt{Grant} supplies the lineage
and constraints; \texttt{World} offers delegation, action, queue, revocation, report
and resolution methods. A production deployment must implement the richer mandate
and authenticated role separation rather than treating strings as credentials.

For an integration test, submit the same five events to an isolated broker, retain
its actual decisions and reconcile all resulting resource changes. An implementation
fails this contract if a queued request or sibling retains the revoked ancestor's
power, or if historical expenditure disappears. This procedure is executable without
simulating a malicious personality or assuming an agent's willingness to cooperate.

Specialized services could share this mandate interface: payments would require both
transaction authority and remaining organizational budget. A corporate title alone
would not authorize the effect. Implementation and legal validity remain future work.

\section{Relation to Pacing the Frontier}
Amodei's September essay proposes ongoing external evaluation with access to verify
safety practices and report incidents \citep{amodei2026}. That proposal concerns
frontier development and its governance. Our contribution operates at a different
scale: the authority available to a particular organization during execution.

An operational extension could require additional evidence before a mandate expands
its resources, delegation depth or effect channels. Three distinct events would
then remain visible: an evaluator's assessment, an authorized amendment, and the
broker's enforcement of that amendment. Our current fixtures test constraints and
review, not certification thresholds, evaluator independence or capability growth.
